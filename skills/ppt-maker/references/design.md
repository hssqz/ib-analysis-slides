# Design and expression selection

Preserve user-approved style before considering a preset. These are original public workflow guidelines; no bank, brand, font or slide collection is included here.

## New work

Confirm audience and decision; build a narrative from claims and evidence. Page approval includes complete copy, actual placement in an ASCII sketch, source requirements, color/type hierarchy and editable/media choices. Summaries, covers and appendix pages may use descriptive titles; analytical titles state supported conclusions. Do not inflate sparse material into fabricated evidence.

For a fundraising deck, prioritize customer problem, concrete product, business model, traction, execution and the funding decision as evidence permits. Use a clear visual focus, readable presentation-scale type and restrained brand color. An old deck supplied for images does not supply current facts or a template. Distinguish shipped, tested, planned and unverified claims. Logos/backgrounds come from permitted user assets; no private Webis material is bundled.

For reading-oriented consulting or due diligence, use several complementary evidence views under one question. Use compact aligned tables and charts without reducing the whole page to small text. Keep argument, explanation, evidence and qualification visually distinct. A dense institutional profile supplies its own type scale; generic presentation defaults must not override it. Strong table headers, brackets, colored fills and dividers appear only when their informational role exists.

## Component choices

These twelve relationship guides preserve the former consulting-kit's selection intent. They are not migrated PPTD templates or twelve tested PowerPoint implementations. Author the selected relationship with the new backend and validate it with actual input.

| Relationship | Choose when | Evidence/editability constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Actual/forecast trend | One series continues into projections | Shared boundary point; distinct line treatment and forecast label |
| Compound growth | Positive endpoints over known elapsed periods | Recompute CAGR; do not count labels as periods |
| Scenarios | Consistent optimistic/base/conservative assumptions | Common axes; no invented probabilities |
| Composition snapshot | Mutually exclusive portions of one total | Nonnegative shares, explicit denominator and rounding |
| Composition change | Comparable categories across periods | Keep classification/denominators stable; distinguish amount/share |
| Waterfall bridge | Additive changes reconcile start to end | Verify all steps; subtotal not counted twice; shape chart allowed |
| Benchmark | Comparable companies, products or regions | Same units/period; wrap long labels without deleting qualifiers |
| Sensitivity matrix | Two independent assumptions drive one measure | Complete grid and base case; native cells and readable color scale |
| Priority matrix | Evidence supports two qualitative/numeric axes | Explain axis meaning; distinguish judgment from measurement |
| Value chain | Roles or process relationships matter | Native nodes/connectors; arrows do not invent causality |
| Timeline | Dated events and status matter | Correct chronological positions; future/actual visibly distinct |
| Table plus insight | Financial/operating evidence supports conclusions | Native cells, period/unit headers, subtotal hierarchy; concise commentary |

Facts and visual relationships govern category count. Old fixed node counts are not universal limits. If source content exceeds a layout, regroup/reflow/split before shrinking. Review actual longest labels, negative values, missing observations and changed periods. Keep missing values distinct from zero; business revenue does not establish business profit contribution without appropriate costs/profit evidence.
